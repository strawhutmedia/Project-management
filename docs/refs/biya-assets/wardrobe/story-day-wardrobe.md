# BACK IN YOUR ARMS (BIYA) — Story-Day Wardrobe Breakdown

**Source:** `biya-screenplay.txt` (BIYA_3.0.fdx dump, 157 scenes parsed)
**Prepared for:** costume continuity / script supervision
**Method:** Read start-to-finish in scene order. Story days segmented by DAY/NIGHT tags, sleep/overnight gaps, and explicit time cues ("the next day," "days later," "time passes," "two weeks later"). Outfits numbered by **story day**, not shooting day.

---

## IMPORTANT STRUCTURAL FINDINGS (read first)

1. **There is essentially ONE present timeline that relocates**, not two intercut timelines. The film runs **Minneapolis (winter) → Solvang (warm) → Minneapolis (winter)** *sequentially*. The "Minneapolis earlier life" (Sawyer's affair with David, who was her sister Anna's fiancé) is **backstory revealed only in dialogue** — it is never dramatized. I still label days `MPLS-` / `SOLV-` per the brief because the climate change (heavy winter coats vs. sundress weather) is a hard costume line.

2. **David has NO on-screen wardrobe. There are NO flashback / memory / hallucination scenes in this draft.** David (Sawyer's dead husband) exists only as: Scene 2 V.O., a phone photo (Sc 14), dialogue, and one physical artifact — the **"DILL WITH IT" pickle-in-sunglasses T-shirt** that Sawyer keeps and handles throughout. So the anticipated "Memory set (M1, M2…)" for David **does not exist**. If production later adds David flashbacks, they are not in this script. → **Costume decision flag.**

3. **Scene 1 is an undated stylistic prologue (cold open), not a datable story day.** It is the break-in/intruder motif shot at **Jason Kendrick's house** ("the home we recognize from the OPENING SEQUENCE," confirmed Sc 73). Deliberately faceless: a man's **BOOTS**, and a second escaping figure seen only as **shoes + jeans**. In hindsight the escaping figure reads as **Margo**. **DIRECTOR'S CALL (Ryan): Scene 1 is its OWN day with UNIQUE outfits — NOT the same day as, and NOT reusing, the stab-night looks.** Numbered separately: **Kendrick #17, Margo #6** (was mistakenly #14 / #5).

4. **"Jack Robbins" is Jason Kendrick's character on the show-within-the-film, *XenoSouls*.** His noir-detective TV costume is a **separate production wardrobe** from Kendrick's real-life clothes, and appears only on screens (Sawyer's TV/phone/YouTube: Sc 7, 13, 16, 24, 29, 98, 106, 160). Numbered separately as **JK-TV**.

5. **Scene numbering in the source is imperfect** — scene numbers 22, 125, 148, 154 are absent, and 149/150, 155/157 appear out of order. Assignments below use the scene numbers as printed.
