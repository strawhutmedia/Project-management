# Back In Your Arms — work authorization for foreign cast

Planning note, 2026-09-17. **Not budgeted.** Raised when Ilene Feldman's offer
to Holliday Grainger (UK) for the lead role of Sawyer put the question on the
table. Applies to any non-US cast member, not just this one.

## The visa is O-1B

Extraordinary ability in the arts — the standard route for a foreign actor on a
US production.

P-2 is not available. The UK reciprocal exchange runs Actors' Equity ↔ British
Equity and is **stage only**; SAG-AFTRA's P-2 exchange is with ACTRA in Canada.

## Cost

Straw Hut is under 25 full-time employees, so the small-employer rate applies to
two line items.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Form I-129 petition (small employer, ≤25 FTE) | $530 |
| Asylum Program Fee (small employer) | $300 |
| Premium processing, I-907 — 15 business days | $2,965 |
| Consular visa fee, London embassy | $205 |
| Visa Integrity Fee | $250 |
| SAG-AFTRA consultation letter | usually no charge |
| **Government subtotal** | **~$4,250** |
| Immigration attorney | $5,000–10,000 |
| **Realistic all-in** | **$9,000–14,000** |

The attorney is the cost, not the government. An O-1B is an evidence-built
petition — press, awards, credits, salary comparables, testimonial letters.

Against a cast line of $123k all-in, one of these is another **8–11%**.

Caveat on the Visa Integrity Fee: signed into law July 2025, applies to visas
issued on or after October 2025, but collection has been uneven across consulates
with no firm universal start date. Budget it; it may not be charged.

## Ask these two questions before accepting the number

**1. Does she already hold a current O-1?** Working British actors often have one
filed through a US agent rather than a single employer, and an agent-filed
petition can cover multiple engagements across different productions. If one
covers the shoot window, this is an amendment or nothing — $14,000 becomes near
zero. Her US team knows immediately. (Ryan's read: if she had one, Ilene would
not have asked. Worth confirming anyway; it costs one email.)

**2. Should her agent petition instead of us?** The petitioner does not have to
be Straw Hut. An agent-filed O-1 covers multiple employers, belongs to her, and
serves her next US job — which changes who has reason to carry the cost. Put
that back to Ilene rather than a flat yes or no.

## Two weeks of shooting costs the same as two years

It is a petition, not a per-day permit, and an O-1 can be granted for **up to
3 years**. So build the itinerary to cover reshoots, ADR, press, the premiere and
festival appearances. Same cost, far more use, and no second filing when she is
needed for publicity.

## Hire an immigration attorney, not the entertainment attorney

Different bar specialty, different work. They do different jobs on this:

- **Entertainment attorney** — her deal memo, SAG paperwork, the contract the
  petition attaches to.
- **Immigration attorney** — builds and files the O-1B.

Ask for one who does **O-1B for performers specifically**. It is a real niche:
they know what USCIS accepts as evidence for an actor and they hold the
SAG-AFTRA consultation relationship. A generalist who mostly files H-1Bs for
tech will be slower and cost more. The entertainment attorney almost certainly
knows who to call — take the referral rather than searching cold.

## Timeline — the harder constraint

**6–10 weeks from "go" to visa in hand**, most of it before USCIS sees the file.

Premium processing buys 15 business days *at USCIS*, and that clock starts at
filing. Before filing: the attorney builds the evidence package, the SAG-AFTRA
consultation letter is obtained, and a signed deal memo or contract is attached.
After approval: a consular appointment in London, then the interview.

Skipping premium processing saves $2,965, but regular processing swings to
months. On a shoot with locked dates and a cast, that is not a risk worth $3k.

Work backwards from the target shoot window now.

## On who pays

There is no legal requirement that the employer pays for an O-1 — unlike H-1B,
where some fees must be the employer's. It is a negotiating point, not an
obligation. Ilene asking means it is on the table either way.
